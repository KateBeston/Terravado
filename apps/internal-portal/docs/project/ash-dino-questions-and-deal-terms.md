# Ash & Dino — wedge-validation questions and deal terms to agree

_Last updated: 26 Oct 2026. Working notes, not legal advice._

---

## Part A — Questions to test the wedge (for Ash and Dino)

Goal: find out whether the gap Terravado fills is real, painful and worth paying for —
**before** building more. These are diagnostic, not a pitch. The strongest signal is them
describing the pain *before you name it*, so ask, then listen. Don't lead them to the answer
you want.

1. **When a client asks "can I actually afford this, and what will it really cost me to hold?",
   what do you do right now?** What tools, spreadsheets or people do you reach for?
   _(Reveals the current workflow and where the gaps are.)_

2. **Which part of that do you dread or rebuild for every client?** Where does it eat your time
   or break down? _(Pain, and whether it's recurring enough to pay to fix.)_

3. **You can get HtAG-grade market data. What can't it answer for you that you still have to
   work out separately?** _(Tests the wedge directly — does the data stop short of the client's
   financial reality?)_

4. **When a broker, accountant and buyers agent are all on one client's deal, where do things
   fall through the cracks?** _(Tests the "whole team / coordinate the journey" angle.)_

5. **Have you ever paid for software that promised to help here? What made you keep it — or drop
   it?** _(Willingness to pay, and what kills a tool.)_

6. **How do you currently show a client the recommendation actually stacks up — serviceability,
   after-tax hold, structure? Has that ever caused you a problem?** _(Tests the evidence /
   compliance value, and client-trust angle.)_

7. **If a tool did that well, would you pay monthly — and what would it need to save or make you
   to be worth it?** _(Price anchoring. Ask this late, grounded in the pain they've described —
   not up front.)_

8. **What would make you NOT trust or NOT use a tool like this?** _(Surfaces objections and
   deal-breakers early.)_

> Listen for problems they raise unprompted. Enthusiasm for your idea is weak signal; an
> unprompted description of the exact pain is strong signal.

## Part B — What to agree with Ash before the work deepens

Still exploratory — this is the checklist to walk into the conversation with, so nothing
important stays vague. See a lawyer before signing anything with equity in it.

1. **Ownership.** Do you hold 100%, or does Ash take equity? Decide which — they're different
   deals. "Own it *and* maybe equity" is the vague version to avoid.

2. **If equity:** how much, *vesting* over what period, and tied to what ongoing contribution
   (not a one-off knowledge dump). A cliff and a vesting schedule protect you if it stalls.

3. **His role, named:** co-founder, paid domain consultant, or advisor with a small stake.
   Pick one.

4. **Free access, scoped:** free for his *personal* investing is an easy gift. Running his
   *business* on it is commercial use — that belongs in the equity deal or on commercial terms,
   not "free forever."

5. **His contribution, defined:** what exactly he gives — domain review of the logic, intros,
   pilot client access, time — and over what period.

6. **Decision rights:** who decides on product, direction and money when you two disagree.

7. **IP and origin:** confirm in writing that Terravado's IP is yours, given it began as an
   example for his business. Get that acknowledged clean, now.

8. **Exit:** what happens to equity, access and IP if it ends or sours. Plan it calmly at the
   start, not later in the heat.

> Clean and explicit early is what lets the expertise be an asset instead of a future tangle.
